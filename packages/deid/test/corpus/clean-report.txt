Client A is a woman in her late twenties who referred herself after about six months of low mood. She describes feeling flat in the evenings, sleeping poorly and withdrawing from friends. She works full time in an office role and lives with family.

She reports two earlier periods of low mood in her teens. On both occasions she did not tell anyone how bad things were. The current period began after a relationship ended.

In the first four sessions we agreed a frame and a first goal in her own words: to stop feeling that she has to be fine all the time. She is polite and quick to agree, and she apologises often. I noticed that I prepared more than usual for her sessions, and I took this to supervision.

Formulation, as a hypothesis: she learned early that showing distress would be treated as a burden, so she hides it and looks after others. As a result nobody knows what she needs, she feels alone, and this confirms the belief. A close friend and an interest in drawing are protective.

Plan: one small disclosure to her friend each week, written predictions, and a return to drawing. I will watch for bad weeks that she does not mention. Questions for supervision: how to respond when she reports improvement to please me, and how to raise the pattern without it sounding like criticism.
